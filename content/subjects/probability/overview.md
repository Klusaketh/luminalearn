Probability is the mathematics of uncertainty. It gives precise answers to questions such as how likely a test result is to be correct, how many requests a server should expect per second, or how confident a model should be in its prediction. Probability is the foundation of statistics, machine learning, cryptography, queueing theory, and the randomised algorithms used throughout computer science.

This subject starts with sample spaces, events, and the axioms of probability, then develops conditional probability, independence, and Bayes' theorem, including the classic examples where intuition fails. It introduces random variables and their distributions, and the expectation, variance, and moments that summarise them. The distributions module covers the discrete Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, and Poisson distributions and the continuous uniform, exponential, and normal distributions, along with when each one arises. The final modules study several random variables together, with joint distributions, covariance, and correlation, and end with the law of large numbers and the probability inequalities of Markov and Chebyshev.

Every lesson includes worked numerical problems and small simulations, so you can check an exact answer against what actually happens when you repeat an experiment many times.

## What you will learn
- Define sample spaces and events and apply the axioms of probability
- Compute conditional probabilities and test events for independence
- Apply Bayes' theorem, including to diagnostic testing problems
- Describe random variables with probability mass, density, and cumulative distribution functions
- Calculate expectation, variance, and higher moments, and use linearity of expectation
- Recognise and use the Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, and Poisson distributions
- Work with the uniform, exponential, and normal distributions
- Describe joint distributions and compute covariance and correlation
- State the law of large numbers and bound probabilities with Markov's and Chebyshev's inequalities

## Glossary
- **Sample space**: the set of all possible outcomes of an experiment
- **Event**: a subset of the sample space
- **Conditional probability**: P(A | B), the probability of A given that B has occurred
- **Independence**: events A and B are independent if P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B)
- **Bayes' theorem**: P(A | B) = P(B | A) · P(A) / P(B), for reversing a conditional probability
- **Random variable**: a function that assigns a number to each outcome
- **PMF and PDF**: the probability mass function (discrete) and probability density function (continuous)
- **Expectation**: the long-run average value of a random variable
- **Variance**: the expected squared distance of a random variable from its mean
- **Binomial distribution**: the number of successes in n independent trials with the same success probability
- **Normal distribution**: the bell-shaped continuous distribution described by a mean and a standard deviation
- **Covariance**: a measure of how two random variables vary together
- **Law of large numbers**: the result that sample averages approach the expected value as the sample grows
