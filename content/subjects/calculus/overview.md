Calculus is the mathematics of change and accumulation. Derivatives measure how fast a quantity changes, and integrals add up infinitely many small contributions. Together they describe motion, growth, rates, areas, and optimisation, and they are essential in physics, engineering, economics, and machine learning, where gradient descent uses derivatives to train every neural network.

This subject begins with limits and continuity, the foundation on which derivatives are defined. It develops differentiation rules, including the chain rule, and applies derivatives to rates of change, curve sketching, and optimisation. The integration modules introduce definite and indefinite integrals, the fundamental theorem of calculus that links them to derivatives, and the techniques of substitution and integration by parts, with applications to areas and accumulated quantities. Sequences and series lead to Taylor expansions, which approximate functions with polynomials. The multivariable module extends derivatives to functions of several variables, with gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians, and the subject closes with first-order differential equations.

Each lesson pairs the rules with a worked application, such as finding the optimal dimensions of a container or computing a gradient step.

## What you will learn
- Evaluate limits and decide whether a function is continuous
- Differentiate functions with the product, quotient, and chain rules
- Use derivatives to find rates of change, sketch curves, and solve optimisation problems
- Compute definite integrals and apply the fundamental theorem of calculus
- Integrate with substitution and integration by parts, and compute areas and accumulated totals
- Test sequences and series for convergence and build Taylor series approximations
- Compute partial derivatives, gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians, and use them in optimisation
- Solve separable and linear first-order differential equations

## Prerequisites
- Algebra and functions: linear, quadratic, exponential, and logarithmic functions
- Trigonometry basics: sine, cosine, and the unit circle
- Comfort with graphs of functions

## Glossary
- **Limit**: the value a function approaches as its input approaches a point
- **Continuity**: a function is continuous at a point if its limit there equals its value
- **Derivative**: the instantaneous rate of change of a function; the slope of its tangent line
- **Chain rule**: the derivative of a composite function: (f(g(x)))′ = f′(g(x)) · g′(x)
- **Critical point**: a point where the derivative is zero or undefined
- **Integral**: the accumulated total of a quantity; geometrically, signed area under a curve
- **Antiderivative**: a function whose derivative is the given function
- **Fundamental theorem of calculus**: the result linking definite integrals to antiderivatives
- **Taylor series**: an infinite polynomial approximation of a function around a point
- **Partial derivative**: the rate of change of a multivariable function along one variable
- **Gradient**: the vector of partial derivatives, pointing in the direction of steepest increase
- **Hessian**: the matrix of second partial derivatives, describing curvature
- **Differential equation**: an equation relating a function to its derivatives
