Statistics is the science of learning from data in the presence of variation. Probability starts from a known model and predicts data; statistics works the other way, starting from data and drawing careful conclusions about the population or process that produced it. Every A/B test, clinical trial, opinion poll, and machine learning evaluation is a statistical argument.

This subject begins with describing data: measures of centre, spread, and shape, and the graphs that reveal them. It then covers how data is collected and why the sampling method matters as much as the sample size. The sampling module introduces sampling distributions and the central limit theorem, which explains why averages behave so predictably. Estimation follows, with point estimates and confidence intervals. The hypothesis testing modules explain significance tests and p-values, then apply them with t-tests for means and chi-square tests for categorical data. The subject closes with relationships between variables: correlation and simple linear regression.

Lessons stress interpretation as much as calculation: what a confidence interval does and does not mean, and why a small p-value is not proof of an important effect.

## What you will learn
- Summarise data with measures of centre, spread, and shape, and choose suitable graphs
- Distinguish observational studies from experiments and identify sampling bias
- Explain sampling distributions and apply the central limit theorem
- Compute point estimates and construct and interpret confidence intervals
- Set up hypotheses, compute test statistics, and interpret p-values correctly
- Carry out one-sample and two-sample t-tests and chi-square tests
- Measure the strength of linear relationships with correlation
- Fit, interpret, and check a simple linear regression model

## Glossary
- **Population**: the entire group about which conclusions are drawn
- **Sample**: the subset of the population actually observed
- **Statistic**: a number computed from a sample, such as the sample mean
- **Parameter**: a fixed but usually unknown number describing a population
- **Standard deviation**: a measure of how far values typically lie from the mean
- **Sampling distribution**: the distribution of a statistic over all possible samples of a given size
- **Central limit theorem**: the result that sample means are approximately normally distributed for large samples
- **Confidence interval**: a range produced by a method that captures the parameter in a stated proportion of samples
- **Null hypothesis**: the default claim that a significance test tries to find evidence against
- **P-value**: the probability, assuming the null hypothesis, of a result at least as extreme as the one observed
- **Type I error**: rejecting a null hypothesis that is actually true
- **Correlation coefficient**: a number between −1 and 1 measuring the strength of a linear relationship
- **Regression line**: the line that minimises the sum of squared prediction errors
