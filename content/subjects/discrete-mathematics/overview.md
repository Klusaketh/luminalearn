Discrete mathematics studies structures made of separate, countable pieces: integers, sets, logical statements, graphs, and finite arrangements. It is the mathematics of computer science. Logic underpins programming and circuit design, counting arguments determine how long an algorithm runs, graphs model networks and dependencies, and modular arithmetic is the basis of hashing and cryptography.

This subject begins with propositional and predicate logic and the main proof techniques: direct proof, contrapositive, contradiction, and cases. It then covers sets, functions, and cardinality, and relations, with a focus on equivalence relations and the partitions they create. The combinatorics modules teach the sum and product rules, the pigeonhole principle, permutations and combinations, and the binomial theorem. Mathematical induction is treated in depth, together with recurrence relations and how to solve them. The subject closes with graph theory, covering paths, connectivity, trees, and Euler and Hamilton circuits, and with number theory: divisibility, the Euclidean algorithm, primes, and modular arithmetic.

Lessons emphasise writing clear, correct arguments, and each worked example shows a proof or a count laid out step by step.

## What you will learn
- Translate English statements into propositional and predicate logic and evaluate them
- Write direct proofs, proofs by contrapositive, proofs by contradiction, and proofs by cases
- Work with sets, functions, and cardinality, including countable and uncountable sets
- Identify equivalence relations and the partitions they induce
- Count arrangements with the sum and product rules, permutations, and combinations
- Apply the pigeonhole principle and the binomial theorem
- Prove statements with weak and strong induction
- Set up and solve recurrence relations
- Describe graphs with standard terminology and reason about paths, trees, and circuits
- Compute GCDs with the Euclidean algorithm and work with primes and modular arithmetic

## Prerequisites
- School algebra
- No previous experience with proofs is required
- Some programming experience helps when connecting topics to algorithms

## Glossary
- **Proposition**: a statement that is either true or false
- **Predicate**: a statement containing variables that becomes a proposition once values are given
- **Quantifier**: "for all" (∀) or "there exists" (∃), stating how many values satisfy a predicate
- **Proof by contradiction**: assuming a statement is false and deriving an impossibility
- **Set**: an unordered collection of distinct objects
- **Bijection**: a function that is both one-to-one and onto
- **Equivalence relation**: a relation that is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive
- **Permutation**: an ordered arrangement of objects
- **Combination**: an unordered selection of objects
- **Pigeonhole principle**: if more than n objects go into n boxes, some box holds at least two
- **Induction**: proving a statement for a base case and showing each case implies the next
- **Recurrence relation**: an equation defining each term of a sequence from earlier terms
- **Graph**: a set of vertices joined by edges
- **Congruence**: a ≡ b (mod n) means n divides a − b
