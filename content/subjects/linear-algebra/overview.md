Linear algebra is the study of vectors, matrices, and the linear transformations they represent. It is the working language of computer graphics, machine learning, data compression, search ranking, and scientific computing. A photo is a matrix, a 3D rotation is a matrix, and training a neural network is mostly matrix multiplication; linear algebra explains what those operations mean and when they can be undone.

This subject begins with systems of linear equations and solves them with Gaussian elimination, then introduces determinants and matrix inverses. It develops vector spaces and matrix multiplication, followed by the central ideas of linear independence, basis, dimension, and rank. Linear transformations connect matrices to geometry: rotations, reflections, projections, and scalings. The orthogonality module covers dot products, orthogonal projections, the Gram-Schmidt process, and least-squares solutions to systems with no exact answer. The subject closes with eigenvalues and eigenvectors, diagonalisation, and the singular value decomposition, the factorisation behind principal component analysis and low-rank approximation.

Each lesson combines hand calculations on small matrices with a geometric picture of what the calculation does.

## What you will learn
- Solve systems of linear equations with Gaussian elimination and row-echelon form
- Compute determinants and inverses and use them to decide whether a system has a unique solution
- Multiply matrices and interpret the product as composing transformations
- Decide whether vectors are linearly independent and find a basis and the dimension of a space
- Compute the rank of a matrix and relate it to the solutions of a system
- Represent rotations, reflections, projections, and scalings as matrices
- Project vectors onto subspaces, orthogonalise a basis, and solve least-squares problems
- Find eigenvalues and eigenvectors and diagonalise a matrix
- Compute and interpret the singular value decomposition

## Prerequisites
- School algebra, including solving simultaneous equations
- Familiarity with coordinates and vectors in two and three dimensions helps
- No calculus is required

## Glossary
- **Vector**: an ordered list of numbers, or an arrow with length and direction
- **Matrix**: a rectangular array of numbers representing a linear transformation or a system of equations
- **Gaussian elimination**: row operations that reduce a matrix to row-echelon form
- **Determinant**: a number that is zero exactly when a square matrix is not invertible
- **Inverse matrix**: the matrix A⁻¹ with AA⁻¹ = I
- **Vector space**: a set closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication
- **Linear independence**: no vector in the set is a linear combination of the others
- **Basis**: a linearly independent set that spans the space
- **Rank**: the dimension of the column space of a matrix
- **Orthogonal**: perpendicular; two vectors are orthogonal when their dot product is zero
- **Least squares**: the solution that minimises the squared error of an inconsistent system
- **Eigenvector**: a nonzero vector v with Av = λv; its direction is unchanged by A
- **Singular value decomposition (SVD)**: the factorisation A = UΣVᵀ of any matrix
